{\Large\bfseries L06 — Truss size optimisation}

{\itshape No new theory: L06 = the FEA $+$ L05's adjoint $+$ L04's fmincon, in one loop.}

\heading{1. The physical problem}
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \min_{A_1,\dots,A_{n_e}}\quad & f = \mathbf{f}^{\!\top}\mathbf{u}(a) && \text{compliance}\\
  \text{s.t.}\quad & \textstyle\sum_e L_e A_e \le V && \text{volume budget}\\
  & A_{\min}\le A_e \le A_{\max} && \text{side constraints}\\
  & K(a)\,\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{f} && \text{the FEA}
\end{aligned}
\]
Minimising compliance $=$ maximising stiffness. The optimiser chooses {\itshape areas} of a
fixed candidate set; it never moves a node and never invents a bar.

\heading{2. The element — why area is special}
A bar is axial-only, stiffness $EA/L$ rotated into global axes. The area enters as a pure
multiplier:
\[
  K_e = A_e\,K_e^{\text{unit}},\qquad
  K_e^{\text{unit}} = \frac{E}{L_e}
  \begin{pmatrix}
    l^2 & lm & -l^2 & -lm\\
    lm & m^2 & -lm & -m^2\\
    -l^2 & -lm & l^2 & lm\\
    -lm & -m^2 & lm & m^2
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad \frac{\partial K_e}{\partial A_e} = K_e^{\text{unit}}
\]
with $l=(x_j-x_i)/L_e$, $m=(y_j-y_i)/L_e$ and
$\text{dofs}_e = [\,2i\!-\!1,\;2i,\;2j\!-\!1,\;2j\,]$.
Assemble by superposition, delete the fixed rows/columns, solve
$u_{\text{free}}=K_{\text{free}}\backslash f_{\text{free}}$ — {\bfseries one} system per
objective call.

\heading{3. Three facts before optimising}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The volume constraint costs no analysis — $\sum_e L_e A_e$ has no $u$ in it. One linear row.
\item $V$ is a {\itshape total} volume, never per-bar. ``Every bar at $10^{-3}$'' quietly hands
      a finer grid $25\times$ more steel and every comparison becomes meaningless.
\item \textcolor{good}{The problem is convex} — compliance has a positive semidefinite Hessian
      in $a$, the constraint is linear. Local $=$ global; the start point does not matter.
\end{enumerate}

\heading{4. Non-dimensionalisation — three steps}
Physical units span $\sim\!13$ decades ($A\sim10^{-7}$, $f\sim10^{3}$, $\partial f/\partial A\sim10^{6}$).
\[
\textbf{(1)}\quad A_e = A_{\min} + (A_{\max}-A_{\min})\,x_e,\qquad x_e\in[0,1]
\]
so the side constraint becomes just $0\le x_e\le 1$. $A_{\min}>0$ (not $0$) keeps $K$
non-singular while still meaning ``void''.
\[
\textbf{(2)}\quad \hat f(x) = \frac{f(a(x))}{f_0},\qquad f_0 = f\!\left(a(0)\right)
\]
so $\hat f$ starts at exactly $1$ and reads as a fraction of the start.
\[
\textbf{(3)}\quad
\frac{\sum_e L_e A_e}{V}\le 1
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\underbrace{\frac{A_{\max}-A_{\min}}{V}L^{\!\top}}_{\textstyle A_{\text{ineq}}}x
\;\le\;
\underbrace{1-\frac{A_{\min}\sum_e L_e}{V}}_{\textstyle b_{\text{ineq}}}
\]
\textcolor{warn}{Not cosmetic:} non-dimensionalised $\Rightarrow$ exitflag 1; in raw areas
$\Rightarrow$ exitflag 2 (step size collapsed). fmincon's tolerance is {\itshape relative}.

\heading{5. The sensitivity — free, because compliance is self-adjoint}
\[
  K(a)u=\mathbf{f},\quad f=\mathbf{f}^{\!\top}u
  \;\Longrightarrow\;
  K^{\!\top}\lambda=-\mathbf{f}
  \;\overset{K=K^{\top}}{\Longrightarrow}\;
  \lambda=-u
\]
\[
  \boxed{\;\frac{\partial f}{\partial A_e} = -\,u_e^{\!\top}K_e^{\text{unit}}u_e \;\le\; 0\;}
\]
($u_e$ = bar $e$'s own four DOFs; the quantity is twice a strain energy, hence $\ge0$.)
Chain down to the solver's variable:
\[
  \frac{\partial\hat f}{\partial x_e}
  = \underbrace{\frac{1}{f_0}}_{\text{step 2}}\cdot
    \underbrace{\left(-u_e^{\!\top}K_e^{\text{unit}}u_e\right)}_{\text{adjoint}}\cdot
    \underbrace{\left(A_{\max}-A_{\min}\right)}_{\text{step 1}}
  = -\,\frac{A_{\max}-A_{\min}}{f_0}\,u_e^{\!\top}K_e^{\text{unit}}u_e
\]
The gradient for all $n_e$ bars costs {\bfseries no extra solve} — that is what makes $1296$
variables routine.

\heading{6. Consequence of the sign}
Thickening any bar can only lower compliance, and nothing in the objective stops the optimiser
thickening everything. Therefore
\[
  \text{the volume constraint is \textcolor{good}{active at every optimum}, and }\lambda_{\text{ineqlin}}>0 .
\]
If your run prints a slack budget or $\lambda=0$, the constraint is wired wrong.

\heading{7. Interpreting — five checks}
\begin{enumerate}
\item exitflag (1 = KKT met; 2 = step died; 0 = out of iterations), then
      \texttt{output.iterations}, then \texttt{output.firstorderopt}.
\item $\lambda_{\text{ineqlin}}$ — active? what is material worth?
\item Area histogram, log axis. Void rule: $A_e < 0.01\max_e A_e$.
\item The layout — trace a load path. Top chord tension, bottom chord compression: that couple
      carries the global bending moment, every bar still axial-only.
\item Ground structure and refinement.
\end{enumerate}

\heading{8. Why 'full' must win, and when refinement must}
{\bfseries Connectivity} — a set inclusion, not an observation: every 'neighbour' design is a
'full' design with the long bars parked at $A_{\min}$, so
\[
  \mathcal{F}_{\text{neigh}}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\text{full}}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  f^{*}_{\text{full}} \le f^{*}_{\text{neigh}} .
\]
{\bfseries Refinement} — guaranteed only when the grids {\itshape nest}. $7\times5 \to 13\times9$
halves both spacings, so every old node and bar survives: $f^*$ must fall. $11\times5$ vs
$13\times5$ do not nest and $f^*$ can rise — different feasible sets, not a solver failure.

\heading{9. What convexity does and does not promise}
Promises: the returned {\itshape areas} are a global optimum.
Does not promise: that the {\itshape layout} is the best layout — the answer can only be a
subset of the bars you offered at nodes you fixed. That gap is where topology optimisation
begins.
